## 1. What the owner asked for

> *"can I just introduce a matrix multiplicator M(current quaternion rotation): at
> the moment of grab, the multiplication is reset to identity. Then, according to
> the value of quaternion rotation on each of the 4 quaternion axis, the
> multiplicator M multiplies the current quaternion to correct the bias."*

Plus two questions: **what contaminates yaw — roll, pitch, both?** and **is it
depth-dependent?**

## 2. ⭐ THE ARCHITECTURE IS TEXTBOOK, AND THE LITERATURE NAMES IT

Correcting an attitude by **multiplying** a small correction onto a nominal
estimate — rather than adding — is the **multiplicative error quaternion** of the
**MEKF (Multiplicative Extended Kalman Filter)**, standard in satellite attitude
determination and inertial navigation, for exactly the reason it is right here: a
multiplicative update preserves the unit-norm constraint where an additive one
does not. *"Reset to identity at grab"* is the same nominal-state +
multiplicative-correction split, and it composes cleanly with the cube's rotation
already being grab-relative.

⛔ **What the literature CHANGED: swing/twist, not axis-component scaling.** The
first draft scaled the rotation axis's off-vertical components and renormalised —
ad-hoc. The standard tool factors `q = q_swing ⊗ q_twist` about a chosen axis.
Taken about the vertical, **the twist IS the yaw and the swing IS the
contamination**, exactly. Trimming the swing leaves the turn AMOUNT untouched,
which matters because `ORIENTATION_DIAGNOSIS.md` measured the amount as already
fine (gain 1.13) and the **uprightness** as the thing that fails.

⛔ **MANO-based orientation refinement is the mainstream remedy in the literature
and is licence-blocked by `N13`.** It stays out.

## 3. The two questions, MEASURED (`analysis/lean_decomposition.py`)

**Contaminated by BOTH**, pitch ~1.3× roll, over 7511 yaw-like frames:

| off-axis component | mean | mean abs | character |
|---|---|---|---|
| `nx` → toward **pitch** | +0.375 | **0.431** | one-directional bias (0.87) |
| `nz` → toward **roll** | +0.300 | **0.323** | one-directional bias (0.93) |

⭐ The load-bearing part is not the ratio but that **both are one-directional
BIASES relative to the turn direction**, not symmetric noise. A bias is what a
deterministic correction can remove; noise is what killed the three predecessors.

⛔ **NO depth dependence.** Binned **within** each take, the four takes disagree on
the sign of the trend for both axes. ⚠ An earlier pass pooled the takes and *then*
binned — confounding depth with take identity — and duly reported roll as
depth-dependent on numbers that were not even monotone. **Two pooling errors were
made in one session**; the harness now judges within-take by construction.

## 4. ⛔⛔ THE REGRESSION THAT COST A LIVE SESSION — and the owner found it

The correction faded in on the **twist MAGNITUDE** (`min(1, |twist|/15°)`). It
passed its golden vectors, which used **mathematically pure** pitch and roll where
the twist is exactly zero. **A real hand never produces that**: a pitch gesture
carries incidental yaw, cleared the 15° ramp, and had its entire swing damped.

> Owner: *"that creates an issue because pitch and roll are heavily damped as a
> consequence."*

⭐ **The fix is to ask a RATIO, not a magnitude** — how much of *this* rotation is
yaw?

    r = |twist| / (|twist| + |swing|)        0 = pure pitch/roll, 1 = pure yaw
    w = ramp(r, 0.35 → 0.65)

Measured over 9880 rotating frames: yaw-dominant frames sit at **r = 0.805**,
pitch/roll-dominant at **0.191** — barely overlapping. A `pitch 40° + yaw 10°`
gesture is now **byte-unchanged at gain 1** (`6.4e-15°`), and that is a golden
vector so it cannot regress.

⭐⭐ **THE METHOD RULE: a golden vector built from a MATHEMATICALLY PURE input
tests a case the product never sees.** The vectors were green on a build the owner
rejected in one minute. Purity is the wrong idealisation when the defect lives in
the impurity.

## 7. ⭐⭐ WHY 0.66 — a bracket, not a derivation

The owner set it by eye. Checked afterwards against numbers measured **before this
row existed**. `ROLL` is the one axis that never touches world `z`, so its **6.7°**
mean-axis error is the pipeline's accuracy floor; the lean at a 60–90° turn is
**26.8°**:

    gain landing exactly on the floor = 1 - 6.7/26.8 = 0.750

| gain | residual lean | |
|---|---|---|
| 0.11 | 23.9° | leaves the bias in |
| **0.66** | **9.1°** | **just above the floor** |
| 0.75 | 6.7° | at the floor |
| 1.00 | 0.0° | below it — correcting noise |

⛔ **THE FLOOR IS NOT ONE NUMBER.** The lean grows faster than the turn, so the
floor gain rises with turn size: **0.015 at 20°, 0.455 at 40°, 0.694 at 60°, 0.750
at 75°.** A single scalar cannot sit on it everywhere; 0.66 tracks the large-turn
end, which is where the defect is visible.

⚠ **AND A FIRST MODEL WAS REFUTED BY THE OWNER'S EYE.** Least squares on a
bias-plus-genuine-tilt model gives `g = σ²_bias/(σ²_bias+σ²_genuine) = 0.114`. That
assumed the swing's VARIATION is genuine tilt worth keeping. Tested: its
frame-to-frame change over its spread is **0.11**, not ~1.4 — so it is **not white
noise**, it is a persistent pose-dependent property, and the diagnosis says world
`z` is fabricated *as a function of pose*. ⭐ So the variation is artifact too,
which is exactly why the right gain is far above 0.114.

⚠ **What cannot be claimed**: 6.7° is a mean-axis *error* and the residual lean is
a systematic *tilt* — related, not identical. The argument brackets the answer to
roughly **0.5–0.75**; it does not single out 0.66. ⛔ One route was tried and
closed: using the card takes to estimate genuine tilt — `REJECTED.md` records the
card *reads tilt higher* (17–19° vs 12.6–13.0°) and must never size an axis.
