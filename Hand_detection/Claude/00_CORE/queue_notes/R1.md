## 2. ⭐⭐ The findings worth keeping

### 2.1 The occlusion asymmetry was OUR logic, not MediaPipe's `z`

The owner: *"in the palm and 80 degrees yaw tilted hand, the fingertips are all in
front of the cube except the thumb: this cannot be, since the cube should follow
the barycenter of the fingertips."* Correct, and the cause was an inconsistency
nobody had noticed:

```
cube x,y  =  the FINGERTIP barycentre     (F1)
cube z    =  a PALM-derived depth         (palm_depth, spans 0-5-9-13-17)
```

The object sat at the fingertips **laterally** and at the palm's depth **axially**.
Gripping palm-forward the fingers wrap toward the camera — measured **3.6 cm
nearer** than the hand origin — so every tip landed in front of the cube. Turn the
hand over and the same fingers are **3.7 cm farther**, so they all landed behind it
and it looked right. Same code, opposite appearance.

⭐ Fixed by anchoring `cube.grab_hand_depth_m` on the grip point. ⚠ No jump at grab:
`grab_depth_offset_m` is measured against the same value, so the two shift together
and cancel at t=0.

### 2.2 ⛔⛔ TWO RETRACTIONS ON THE WAY THERE

1. *"MediaPipe puts palm-forward fingertips on the wrong side of z."* **WRONG.** It
   came from a take containing **two hands**, split by `signed_palm_area` — whose
   sign is **chirality-dependent**. The split was really *side XOR handedness*. On
   both single-hand takes the sign is correct: −0.036 m palm-forward, +0.037 m
   back-forward. ⚠ Exactly the error class `U7` records, committed again.
2. The whole-hand **depth-reversal** hypothesis, tested in
   `analysis/z_depth_flip.py` and **refuted**: geometric chirality is 100%
   consistent across palm and back frames of the same hand, and a depth reversal
   would flip it. ⭐ The harness needs no ground truth — a hand cannot change
   chirality.

### 2.3 ⭐ What the harnesses found about YAW, for free

The palm quad's own `z` spread is **0.0658 m face-on and 0.0681 m turned ~90°** —
essentially constant. A face-on palm should read ~0 and a turned one ~its own
width. **So Horn's `z` input carries almost no yaw information at all.** That is
`T6`'s "a face-on palm reads as 24.9° tilted" reached by an independent route, and
it is the yaw lean's root. ⛔ It also means fixing fingertip depth cannot help yaw:
`Horn` fits `PALM_LANDMARKS = (0,5,9,13,17)` and the fingertips are not in it.

## 3. The damper: three rejected designs before one stuck

⭐ **Damping is not stillness.** Even at 4500 ms of extra tau the blend factor is
0.015, so the cube still creeps 1.5% of the gap every frame and wanders over a long
hold. That realisation is what moved this from a filter to a **freeze**.

| attempt | verdict |
|---|---|
| raise the fixed tau | ⛔ `L1` already rejected it: lags real motion as much as it damps jitter |
| ADAPTIVE tau (fast-attack speed envelope) | ⛔ owner: *"did not dampen as much as previously"* — the envelope was TRIGGERED BY NOISE (the raw target exceeds 160 °/s on **5.4% of frames** with the hand still) and each spike released the damping for ~350 ms |
| freeze with a **2-frame** trigger | ⛔ owner: *"makes the rotation jerky"* — and measured: slow-turn following 77.6% → 58.9% |
| **freeze with a 1-frame trigger** | ✅ shipped |

### 3.1 ⛔⛔ THE COHERENCE GATE — built twice, removed twice

The owner's idea, and it is a *good measurement*: noise reverses direction between
frames, real motion persists. Built first per landmark, then — at the owner's
suggestion — as a whole-hand matrix operation over frames N, N+1, N+2.

| measure | still | slow turn | separation |
|---|---|---|---|
| per-landmark sign vote | 0.353 | 0.810 | 0.98 |
| **Frobenius correlation** `⟨D₁,D₂⟩/(‖D₁‖‖D₂‖)` | **−0.260** | **+0.538** | **1.01** |
| dominant-direction energy (Tomasi–Kanade rank) | 0.762 | 0.818 | 0.33 |
| rigid residual (Kabsch fit N→N+2) | 0.831 | 0.856 | 0.12 |

⭐ The Frobenius form is the better measure and has a **principled zero threshold**:
a still hand reads NEGATIVE because consecutive noise steps anti-correlate.

⛔ **And it is still a bad TRIGGER**, which is a property of the question rather
than the implementation: **coherence says the hand is moving *somehow*; the freeze
needs to know *how much*.** A drifting still hand is coherent; a hand pausing
mid-turn is not. On 6038 frames of natural use it cost **7–11 points of slow-turn
following for about 1 point of stillness**, at every setting and in both AND and OR
formulations. As an AND-gate it can only *reduce* releases, so it cannot help the
slow case by construction.

⚠ Both implementations were **computing every frame in both tools with the result
discarded** when off. That is the reason to delete rather than park.

### 3.2 The one dial, measured on 6038 frames of natural use

| `RELEASE` | moves when STILL | follows a SLOW turn |
|---|---|---|
| 50 | 10.2% | 87.2% |
| **60** | **6.0%** | **77.6%** |
| 70 | 3.3% | 70.3% |
| 80 | 1.4% | 62.0% |

⛔ **No setting gives both.** Slow deliberate turns and hand noise occupy the same
speed range — the same overlap that defeated the coherence gate. `RELEASE` *is* the
trade-off.

⭐ **One threshold serves rotation AND translation**, and that was measured, not
assumed: while a cube is held the two distributions nearly coincide (p50 27 deg/s
vs 26 px/s, p75 64 vs 53). ⚠ If the camera or working distance changes, the pixel
figures move and the degree figures do not — **re-measure, do not re-derive.**

## 4. ⚠ Known limits, stated rather than discovered later

* **The object is 7.2 cm; the operator's grip aperture measures ~2 cm.** They are
  not holding the cube, they are pinching it — so no depth placement of a 7.2 cm
  solid puts the fingertips on its surface. With the solid occluder on, holding
  hides every fingertip. ⛔ Not a bug: it is what "solid" plus "centred on the tips"
  jointly mean. The levers are the object's SIZE or moving the near face to the
  grip point (declined by the owner).
* **While frozen the object ignores slow drift**, so a hand creeping below the
  threshold accumulates a gap paid back as a jump on release.
* **Per-landmark occlusion rides on MediaPipe's per-landmark `z`**, which `T6`
  measured as unreliable for ORIENTATION. Ordering is a coarser question, but it is
  the same coordinate: if the skeleton flickers in and out of a cube, that is the
  cause and not the compositing.
