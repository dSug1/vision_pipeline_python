## Appended 2026-08-25 — ⛔⛔ THE MODEL CARD SAYS FINGERTIPS ARE THE WORST LANDMARKS

**Read this before designing `F1`. It was not known when the row was written.**
MediaPipe's own Model Card (archived under `60_SECURITY_COMPLIANCE/evidence/`)
reports, from Google's own fairness evaluation:

> *"We found that per-joint MNAE is the smallest at the base of each finger, and
> gets larger toward the fingertip. We conjecture that the prediction is easier
> around the palm which is more rigid than the fingers."* — and *"The findings
> are consistent across all regions."*

⛔⛔ **`F1` proposes driving the object's whole transform from exactly the
landmarks the model estimates WORST.** That does not kill the row — the owner's
reason for it stands, and the palm genuinely is too coarse for assembly-style
alignment — but it reframes the design problem:

* the palm was not chosen for convenience, it was chosen because **it is rigid,
  and the model is most accurate there**;
* fingertip motion is the signal `F1` wants, and it arrives with **the highest
  per-joint error in the model**;
* ⚠ *"the normalized absolute error is larger for blurry or occluded joints"* —
  and a hand **closing around an object occludes its own fingertips**.

⭐ **So the design must treat tip positions as NOISY MEASUREMENTS, not as truth**
— filtering, contact-consistency, or a bounded trim on top of the palm's coarse
pose, rather than a fit that trusts them equally. ⚠ This is a second, independent
reason not to build a rigid-body fit over palm+tips (the first is that the arm is
A10-dead twice).

⛔⛔ **AND THE CARD PUTS THIS GAME'S CORE ACTION OUT OF SCOPE:**

> *"OUT-OF-SCOPE APPLICATIONS. Not appropriate for: … Predicting hand landmarks
> with gloves or occlusions. **For example when the hand is holding objects** or
> there is decoration on the hand including jewelry, tattoo and henna."*

⚠ **The entire game is a hand holding an object.** That is not a reason to stop —
it demonstrably works well enough to have shipped snap/translate/rotate — but it
is the honest frame for every landmark-quality defect in the queue (`T1`, `T2`,
`N12`), and it means **upstream landmark quality has a ceiling that no estimator
work can lift**. §16.17 already says a jump both estimators reproduce is in the
landmarks; the card says why.

## Appended 2026-08-25 — ⭐⭐ DESIGN DIRECTION: MIX PALM **AND** FINGERTIPS

> **Owner, 2026-08-25:** *"we have already built a lot on palm and we need to use
> that together with fingertips to increase reliability. We shall brainstorm how
> to mix palm and fingertips when we design F1, including our lessons learned on
> palm."*

⛔ **So `F1` is NOT "replace the palm with the fingertips".** It is a **fusion**
design, and the brainstorm is part of the build, not a preliminary. The palm is
not legacy to be superseded — it is the **reliable, low-resolution channel**, and
the tips are the **high-resolution, low-reliability** one.

### ⭐ THE PALM ASSETS THAT MUST BE ON THE TABLE (each one measured, not assumed)

| asset | the evidence |
|---|---|
| **The palm is where the model is most accurate** | per-joint error is *smallest at the finger base, largest at the tip* — *"prediction is easier around the palm which is more rigid than the fingers"* (Model Card) |
| **Horn's five-point averaging is WHY it is stable** | §2.0.2/§2.0.4: **Horn's flaw is BIAS, every per-frame replacement's flaw is VARIANCE.** Averaging over the rigid quad is the stabiliser — do not discard it |
| **`horn-palm` won a six-arm live A/B** | `B4`, 2026-08-17 — palm-rigid beat every alternative anchor |
| ⭐⭐ **The palm normal's BEARING is trustworthy** | §2.0.12: direction error **median 10.6°** vs 45° chance. Only the out-of-plane **magnitude** is corrupt. A reliable bearing is a genuine gift to a fusion design |
| **Depth comes from the palm quad** | `palm_depth`'s ratio form; scale cancels exactly, baselined per grab |
| **Chirality comes from the palm + thumb** | `U7`, 98.0% against a declared hand — and the thumb is what breaks the mirror symmetry, not the palm alone |
| **Conditioning is measurable** | `edge_on_measure`, invariant under pure roll; the foreshortening ratio σ₂/σ₁ is depth-free |

### ⛔ THE PALM LESSONS THAT CONSTRAIN THE MIX

* ⛔⛔ **A rigid-body fit over palm+tips is A10-dead TWICE** (`B4`'s `PALM_AND_TIPS`;
  the 9-point constellation, +1.4° axis for **+4.9° p95 jitter**). Finger motion
  gets fitted as whole-object rotation. **The tips must enter as a DEFORMATION
  relative to the palm frame, or as contact points — never as extra rigid points.**
* ⭐ **Suppress, do not guess.** `DR-2` freezes the sign in the edge-on band, `U8`
  refuses to snap on provisional chirality, `4.2`'s decision 1 does the same. And
  `B8` measured that **holding the last value beats every fit**. A tip that is
  occluded or low-confidence should *drop out of the driver set*, not be
  extrapolated.
* ⚠ **A changing driver SET can itself inject rotation** — a finger joining or
  leaving mid-hold moves the fit. Whatever gates tips in and out must be
  hysteretic, the way `DR-1` is.
* ⚠ **Occlusion is worst exactly when it matters**: *"error is larger for blurry
  or occluded joints"*, and a hand closing on an object occludes its own tips.

### Directions to brainstorm (decide at design time, do not pre-commit)

1. **Palm as FRAME, tips as DEFORMATION** — tips expressed in the palm frame, so
   their motion cannot be read as whole-object rotation by construction.
2. **Bounded fine-TRIM** — palm supplies the coarse pose, tips a limited
   correction with a hard clamp. Fails safe to today's behaviour.
3. **Contact-point model** — tips as grasp contacts on the object; the object
   follows the contacts as a real held object would.
4. **Confidence-weighted fusion** — palm high-reliability/low-resolution, tips
   the reverse; weights driven by measured conditioning, not constants.

⚠ **Whichever wins, `A10` applies in full**, and the bar is
`spec/ROTATION_ACCEPTANCE_AND_TRAPS.md` — all three axes plus **jitter in real
handling**, which is the trap that killed the 9-point constellation.

## Appended 2026-08-25 — ⭐⭐⭐ THE OWNER SPECIFIED IT; THE SPEC IS WRITTEN

⭐ **The design now lives in
[`../../10_HAND_TRACKING/spec/F1_FINGERTIP_TRANSFORM_SPEC.md`](../../10_HAND_TRACKING/spec/F1_FINGERTIP_TRANSFORM_SPEC.md)**
— the owner's eight points verbatim, the architecture, the acceptance bar and the
build order. This dossier keeps only what changed at queue level.

**The owner's specification**: transform follows the **fingertip barycentre** ·
grab when the barycentre is near the object · the tip plane drives the quaternion
with the palm supporting · ⭐ **the back-of-hand snap restriction is REMOVED**,
including on re-entry · release conditions unchanged · a **jitter slider** ·
⭐ **and τ = 20 ms is to be left alone** (stated mid-specification).

### What the spec settled, and how

* ⭐⭐ **The architecture is a palm-frame DEFORMATION feeding a bounded trim** —
  `ΔR = R_palm(t)·R_trim(t)·R_palm(grab)⁻¹`, tips expressed in the palm frame so
  whole-hand rotation cannot enter the tip channel **by construction**. ⛔ At gain
  0 it must be **bit-identical to shipped Horn**, proved by replay — `T6d`'s
  method, and what makes the build revert-free.
* ⭐⭐ **The coplanarity question is answered with its premise inverted.** Coplanar
  tips+palm is the **safe, redundant** case — `R_trim → I` and today's behaviour
  is reached automatically. The dangerous geometry is **COLLINEARITY** (`√(λ₂/λ₁)`
  of the tip cloud), which is a different condition and arises from an edge-on
  extended hand or a two-point pinch. Remedy is the house rule: **suppress, do not
  guess** — fade authority out, hold the last value.
* ⛔ **The owner's "assume z from palm width rotated 90°" is routed to `T6`, not
  built here.** It is the `acos` fold (bogus gains 3.57 / 2.41 / 21.5) plus
  "a threshold must not be computed from a quantity that is noisy where the
  threshold acts" (the adaptive margin collapsed 45% in one frame). ⭐ `T6`'s
  ratio table **is** the disciplined version, and because §3 consumes `R_palm` as
  a black box, `F1` inherits any `T6` win **with no change to the spec**.
* ⛔ **A deadband threshold is refused for the jitter slider** — it converts jitter
  into small pops exactly in the fine-alignment band `F1` exists to serve. The
  **1€ filter** (Casiez/Roussel/Vogel, CHI 2012, BSD-3) replaces it: adaptive
  cutoff, **time-based** — which is `L1`'s lesson restated — two sliders,
  transliterated not imported (`CONSTRAINTS` §2).
* ⛔⛔ **PATENT: do NOT build the contact-point arm.** `US9696795B2` (contacts →
  object rotation, filed 2015-02-19, term ~2035) was **reassigned 2026-01-16 from
  Ultraleap to `SIM IP HXR LLC`**, a holding entity. The chosen design reads on
  Horn 1987 / Kabsch 1976 instead — the same posture that made `T6`'s solver
  1971/1997/2000 prior art.
* ⚠ **`N8` is re-opened** by removing the back-of-hand rule, which had been
  suppressing part of it incidentally. Routed to `B5`/`4.4`, not patched here.
  `U8`'s provisional-chirality gate **stays** — different rule.
* ⭐ **A metric the project does not have is required**: every existing rotation
  metric scores **gross sweep**, so `F1` could pass all of them and deliver
  nothing the owner asked for. A **trim-resolution** measurement is added, with
  the declaration coming from the operator (`B4`'s rule: an anchor metric must not
  share an expression with the anchor).
* ⭐ **Step 0 is measurement, and it is free**: four questions the existing
  415-take corpus answers today (tip confidence during real grabs, the real
  distribution of the tip residual, articulation-induced barycentre drift,
  collinearity frequency). They set the constants instead of taste.

⚠ **Two sub-questions stay deliberately open** — every tip vs. thumb+index, and
grasped-object vs. bounded-trim. ⭐ The chosen form **contains both** (a weight
vector, and the clamp opened up), so the live take decides them rather than an
argument.

## Appended 2026-08-25 — ⭐⭐⭐ STEP 0 MEASURED, and STEP 3 SHIPPED

### The census: `analysis/f1_tip_census.py`

123 takes read, 44 contributing held frames, 59 055 held samples, 192 987
conditioning samples. Tips expressed **in the palm frame** via the shipped
`Horn(PALM_LANDMARKS)`, so the hand's own motion is removed and what is measured
is articulation plus noise.

⛔ **A correction to the spec came out of building it**: the recorder writes only
`landmarks` and `world_landmarks` — **no per-landmark confidence**. Question 1
could not be *"what did MediaPipe think"* and was rewritten as *"what did the tips
actually do"*, split on `cubes[*].owner`. The substitute is better: it scores the
motion that damages the trim rather than the model's opinion of itself.

### M1 — ✅ THE FEARED BLOCKER DID NOT MATERIALISE

⚠⚠ **The raw number was misleading and nearly reported as-is**: frame-to-frame tip
motion is p95 **21–31 mm**, which reads like an unusable channel. Splitting on
frames where the **palm itself barely moved** (< 1°, < 2 mm) shows almost all of
that is the operator genuinely moving:

| | median | p95 |
|---|---|---|
| **noise floor, per tip** | **1.28–1.62 mm** | **3.64–4.71 mm** |

⭐ **Held vs free ratio 1.05–1.10** — the tips *are* slightly noisier while a cube
is held, which is the Model Card's occlusion prediction showing up, **but at
5–10%, not the collapse that would have killed the design.** Thumb worst (1.10),
pinky best (1.08). **A 1.5 mm noise floor is workable.**

### M2 — ⛔ THE RIGID TIP RESIDUAL CANNOT BE USED RAW, AT ANY GAIN

| | p50 | p90 | p95 | p99 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| vs hold start | 30.0° | 77.9° | 96.9° | 147.0° |
| vs ~8 frames (~0.5 s) | 15.9° | 75.3° | 93.0° | 140.4° |

⭐⭐ **The two columns barely differ in the tail, and that is the finding.** If the
excursions were slow re-gripping the short-horizon column would collapse; it does
not. So **75–95° swings happen inside half a second** — and M1 says they are not
sensor noise. They are **the rigid model being wrong**: Horn's "best rigid
explanation" of a non-rigid 5-point set tumbles. ⛔ This is the A10-dead failure
mode reappearing in the *residual* channel, and it means the clamp must sit **far
below** the data's own spread — well under the 16° short-horizon median — or the
fit must be robustified before it is trusted.

### M3 — ⛔ THE PLAIN BARYCENTRE WILL VISIBLY WANDER

| | p50 | p90 | p95 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| vs hold start | 20.0 mm | 61.4 mm | 81.4 mm | 135.6 mm |
| vs ~0.5 s | 9.9 mm | 42.5 mm | 61.6 mm | 138.3 mm |

⭐ **With the palm held still, the fingertip barycentre moves a median 1 cm and a
p95 of 6 cm within half a second.** At `g_pos = 1` — the owner's specification
taken literally — that is the object translating by those amounts from re-gripping
alone. The spec's own estimate (~1.6 cm from one finger) was conservative.
⇒ **`g_pos = 1` needs a clamp, or a gain below 1.** The slider form already spans
both; the live take picks the value.

### M4 — ✅ COLLINEARITY IS RARE, SO THE FREEZE IS CHEAP

`spread = √(λ₂/λ₁)`: p1 **0.172**, p5 0.253, median 0.483, p95 0.754.
`scale = √λ₁/palm_span`: p1 0.700, median 1.553.

| floor | frames frozen |
|---|---|
| 0.10 | 0.07% |
| 0.15 | 0.55% |
| **0.20** | **1.89%** |
| 0.30 | 9.87% |

⇒ **Set the floor at 0.20**: it costs under 2% of frames and is above the p1.

### ⭐ STEP 3 SHIPPED — rule 3's back-of-hand snap block is REMOVED

Both tools, `handinput`, the recorder schema and the conformance traces.
`_thumb_outward_snap_allowed` **deleted**; `_last_known_thumb_outward` **kept** as
an observation (HUD + `palm_facing`). See `20_GAME_RULES/GAME_RULES.md` rule 3 for
the behavioural record and `N8` for the consequence.

**Measured effect** (6 takes, 3432 hand-frames that carry the rule's own recorded
fields): the rule refused on **15.7%** of hand-frames; on **8.3%** the hand held
nothing, so a grab was genuinely blocked. Those are now allowed.

⚠ **Rule 3's behaviour was covered by exactly one test — the scripted conformance
trace.** 26 verification suites passed *before* the removal was finished, which
proves only that they never covered it. The trace's two rule-3 steps are kept and
**inverted**, so they now guard the removal instead.

⚠ **Two latent breaks the change surfaced**, both fixed: `parity_replay` compared

which is real, per-hand and still has to travel; forced on, it passes.

**Verification**: 26/26 suites · conformance regenerated (18 frames, 62 events;

## Appended 2026-08-26 — ⭐ STEP 1 LANDED: the 1€ filter, with golden vectors

`Resources/one_euro.py` + `analysis/verify_one_euro.py` (**30 checks**). Nothing
consumes it yet — that is step 2 — so this landing changes no behaviour at all.

* **Transliterated, not imported.** BSD-3-Clause, attribution carried in the
  module header (`N13`). `import math` and nothing else, so the port contract
  holds (`CONSTRAINTS` §2, verified by an explicit check).
* ⛔ **The off switch is bit-exact** — `enabled=False` returns *the input object*,
  no arithmetic. `F1`'s whole acceptance rests on "off ⇒ identical to today", and
  a passthrough computing `1.0 * x` would make that unprovable. Tested over a
  200-sample stream with `is`, not `==`.
* **Defaults are seeded from the census, not guessed**: `MIN_CUTOFF_HZ = 1.2`,
  `BETA = 0.02`, chosen against the measured **1.5 mm median / 4.7 mm p95**
  fingertip floor. ⚠ They are placeholders until the owner tunes them live.

### ⭐⭐ THE GOLDEN VECTORS CAUGHT A REAL DIVERGENCE ON THE FIRST RUN

The suite carries a **deliberately independent** reference implementation —
different structure, and the *other* algebraic form of alpha (`dt/(tau+dt)` vs
`1/(1+tau/dt)`). Three checks failed immediately, and the cause was substantive:

⛔ **The speed term was built from the last FILTERED value; the published
algorithm uses the last RAW value** (`x->lastRawValue()`). It still converged, and
it still looked correct — which is exactly what would have made it expensive to
find in a JS or Swift port later. Fixed, and the reason is now a comment on
`_LowPass.last_raw` so a port cannot quietly re-introduce it.

⚠ **Had the suite generated its expectations from the implementation, all three
would have passed.** That is this project's own recurring lesson — four harnesses
once reported CLEAN by recomputing what they were meant to check — applied
preventively rather than after the fact.

### ⭐ A test that pins `L1`'s lesson rather than restating it

§4 asserts the filter is **time-based**: the same motion sampled at 15 / 30 / 60
fps reaches the same value after 500 ms (spread **0.013**), *and* the contrast —
the rejected fixed per-frame factor reaches **0.725 at 15 fps vs 0.979 at 60 fps**
after 150 ms. The same constant, two different feels. That is why `L1` replaced it,
now enforced by a check instead of a comment.

§5 pins the reason a deadband was refused: a **sub-threshold drift moves the
output every frame** (no stiction), while a still noisy signal is attenuated to
**32%** of its RMS.

**Verification**: 28/28 suites · `parity_replay` NO DIVERGENCE (1059 frames) ·
stdlib-only confirmed.

## Appended 2026-08-26 — ⭐⭐ STEP 2 BUILT: the object follows the fingertip barycentre

`Resources/fingertips.py` + wiring in **both** tools. ⛔ **Live take owed.**

### What changed

* **Two positions now, and they mean different things.** `palm_pos` is the palm
  centre and is what `handinput` publishes as `palm_pose` — ⛔ **unchanged, on
  purpose**: that action means the palm, and silently redefining a shipped
  action's meaning would break every consumer without altering a signature.
  `hand_pos` is the **filtered fingertip barycentre** and drives snap proximity
  and translation, which is what the owner asked for.
* **Translation follows the grip point**, with the same no-pop construction
  §14.1 and `4.2` each use: the offset is frozen at grab, so the grab frame is
  continuous by construction.
* ⚠ **This replaces §14.1's 9-landmark inverse-distance blend**, which was
  live-verified and is not discarded lightly. ⭐ That blend **already contained
  all five tips**; what changes is the weighting, and with it how much finger
  articulation reaches the object.
* **All five tips or none** — a barycentre that silently changes its denominator
  jumps, which is the adaptive-edge-margin defect in another costume. A missing
  tip **holds** the last good point (`B8`), and falls back to the palm rather
  than to nothing: losing a fingertip is not losing the hand.
* In the debug tool the grip tracker is **upstream and shared across arms**, like
  DR-2's and unlike the per-arm depth trackers — it has no grab dependency, so a
  per-arm copy would add a second variable and destroy the rig's one-variable
  property.

### ⭐ The gate: `analysis/verify_f1_grip_offstate.py`

⭐⭐ **The reference is the RECORDING ITSELF, not a second implementation.** A
production take from before step 2 carries the cube positions the old code
actually produced; replaying it with `USE_TIP_BARYCENTER = False` must reproduce
them. Result: **worst 0.006807 px over 908 comparisons**, against a
recorder-rounding bound of **0.007071** (`round(v, 2)` per axis ⇒ √2 × 0.005).
⚠ **That tolerance is the rounding and nothing else** — loosening it is how a
regression would hide.

⭐ **And the switch is proved LIVE, not just safe**: ON differs on 518 of 908
cube-frames. A gate that passed in both positions would be testing nothing.

⚠⚠ **Two harness bugs found and kept, because both were measuring the wrong
thing convincingly:**

1. It first replayed a **DEBUG-tool** take through **PRODUCTION**. The two are
   kept in step on gesture logic and ownership, *not* on the exact pixel a cube is
   drawn at, so it measured that gap and called it a step-2 regression (6.36 px).
2. The two passes **shared cube state**, so pass 2 began wherever pass 1 left the
   cubes and every frame "differed". ⭐ The giveaway was `moved == compared`
   exactly — frames with nothing held were changing.

### The trim — `Resources/tip_trim.py`

```
q_i(t)    = conj(R_palm) . (tip_i - c_palm) . R_palm     tips IN THE PALM FRAME
R_res(t)  = horn( q_i(grab) -> q_i(t) )                  PURE articulation
R_trim(t) = clamp( gain * fade * R_res )
dR(t)     = R_palm(t) . R_trim(t) . R_palm(grab)^-1
```

* ⭐⭐ **Whole-hand rotation cannot enter the tip channel by construction** — and
  this is now a **test**, not a claim: a rigidly rotated hand produces
  **0.0000° of trim across 20 poses** on four axes at five angles. That property
  is the entire difference between this and `B4`'s `PALM_AND_TIPS`.
* **Frozen at the GRAB**, so `R_trim(grab) = I` and there is no pop — the same
  construction the orientation baseline and `4.2`'s depth ratio each use.
* ⛔ **`TRIM_MAX_DEG = 10°`, deliberately far below the data's own spread.** The
  census measured the rigid tip residual at a **15.9° short-horizon median** and
  75–95° at p90–p95, so this is a *fine trim for alignment*, not "the object
  follows the fingers". A **rate limit** (60°/s) bounds it too, because a
  magnitude clamp alone still allows a full-clamp jump if the fit flips branch.
* **The fade is continuous, never a switch** — a hard gate is itself a rotation
  step. Floors are the census's: `spread ≥ 0.20` (collinearity, 1.89% of frames)
  and a fist-guard on absolute size.
* ⛔ **Gain 0 returns the IDENTITY OBJECT**, so the shipped expression is reached
  byte-for-byte. `TRIM_GAIN` defaults to **0.0** — step 4 lands switched off.

### ⚠ Two fixture bugs in its own test suite, both worth keeping

1. ⭐⭐ **The synthetic hand gave all five tips the SAME reach**, which put them
   nearly on a line: `spread` read **0.172**, just under the floor, so the fade
   correctly zeroed the trim and two checks failed for a reason unrelated to the
   code. Real fingers differ in length, and the census puts a real hand's `spread`
   MEDIAN at **0.483** — the flat fixture was sitting at the **1st percentile of
   reality**. ⚠ A fixture that is accidentally degenerate tests the degeneracy
   handler, not the feature.
2. **Gain proportionality was measured in the SATURATED region** — a 20° curl
   clamps at both gains, so both arms read exactly `TRIM_MAX_DEG` and the check
   would have "passed" while measuring the cap. Moved to 8°, inside the linear
   region, where proportionality is a claim at all.

⭐ The fit itself was correct from the first run: **20.000° recovered from a 20°
synthetic curl**, exact.

| slider | range | start | drives |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SMOOTH ms` | 0–150 | 20 | τ, unchanged |

to lie about its own value — which is the defect that made a panel read "3 ms"
while applying 20 ms. The panel prints **both** tau and the derived Hz, so a take
is readable off a screenshot either way.
