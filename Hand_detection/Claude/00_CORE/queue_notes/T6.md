## Appended 2026-08-25 (corrected) — ⭐⭐ THE 2D-RATIO TABLE IS **NOT** COVERED BY §2.0.12. RETRACTION.

⛔ **The note above maps the owner's path onto §2.0.11/§2.0.12 and concludes it
was already run. That conclusion is WRONG and is retracted here.** The owner
clarified the method and it differs in a way that matters. Kept rather than
deleted, per the house rule.

### The method, as the owner actually specified it

1. Record a **PAUSED** sweep — pure yaw, vertical hand, **8 declared positions**
   0°→180°. Same again for pure pitch.
2. At each declared position compute **inter-landmark distance RATIOS** over the
   **6** palm landmarks. Under pure yaw only the **x**-ratios change; under pure
   pitch only the **y**-ratios.
3. Per frame, compute the same ratios and **match to the table** — yaw from
   x-ratios, pitch from y-ratios, **independently**.
4. The match gives true orientation; the delta from MediaPipe's gives the
   **correction**. Coarse discrete table first, continuous refinement after.

### ⭐⭐ THREE THINGS THAT ARE GENUINELY NEW

1. ⭐⭐⭐ **THE INDEX IS CLEAN, AND THIS IS THE BIG ONE.** §2.0.9 tested transfer
   and refuted it (*worst disagreement **13.4°***) — **but it binned by the FITTED
   pose, which comes from Horn, which is corrupted by the very error being
   modelled.** It indexed the table with a contaminated key. **This method indexes
   by depth-free 2D ratios.** That experiment has not been run, so §2.0.9's
   refutation does **not** carry over to it.
2. ⭐ **YAW AND PITCH STAY SEPARATE.** §2.0.12 had a clean index but collapsed
   tilt into one magnitude + direction — pooling the two axes this project already
   proved *need opposite things from the same coordinate*. Two independent 1-D
   tables preserve that asymmetry **by construction**, where `T6d` had to
   reintroduce it as a fitted sinusoid `g(ψ)`.
3. ⭐ **GROUND TRUTH IS DECLARED, NOT DERIVED.** Operator-named paused positions,
   like the `U7` known-hand take. §2.0.12's `θ_true` came from the analytic
   `σ₂/σ₁ = cos θ`; if that relation is imperfect (non-planar palm, perspective,
   systematic landmark bias) its "truth" was contaminated too.

**The combination — clean index + axes separate + declared empirical table — has
not been tested.** §2.0.12 had the first, §2.0.9 the second, neither the third.

## Appended 2026-08-25 — THE RECORDING PROTOCOL, and why it must be ON-AXIS

> **Owner:** *"I would rather do 3 new records for yaw and 3 new records for pitch
> … taken with the hand strictly aligned with the camera axis. Indeed during
> runtime, the position of the hand in the x and y space … will modify the
> x-dimension and y-dimension ratios due to projection outside of the camera axis
> and will need to be slightly compensated."*

⭐⭐ **CORRECT, AND THE EFFECT IS FIRST-ORDER RATHER THAN SLIGHT.** The ratios are
set by the angle to the **viewing ray**, not to the optical axis. A hand `r` px
from the principal point is seen along a ray tilted by `alpha = atan(r/focal)`.
At 640×480 and the assumed 60° HFOV, `focal_px = 554.26`:

| offset | alpha | as a fraction of a 25° table step |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 25 px | **2.6°** | 10% |
| 50 px | 5.2° | 21% |
| 100 px | 10.2° | 41% |
| 200 px | 19.8° | 79% |
| 250 px | 24.3° | 97% |

⚠ **A palm is ~94 px wide at 0.5 m, so ONE PALM WIDTH off centre is already
~10°.** A table built off-axis bakes that in **unrecoverably**, because alpha is
not stored. Hence: the reference is captured on-axis, and every frame stores its
own offset anyway.

⭐ **And the same relation gives the runtime compensation**, which is simple
precisely *because* yaw and pitch are kept decoupled:

```
yaw_true   ~= yaw_from_table   - atan(x_offset_px / focal_px)
pitch_true ~= pitch_from_table - atan(y_offset_px / focal_px)
```

### The tool: `tools/RecordRatioCalibration.py` (built 2026-08-25)

* **8 declared holds, 0°→180°**, `--axis yaw|pitch`, `--hand left|right` declared
  **before** recording — the `U7` lesson: only a declared physical truth breaks
  the circularity.
* **Live on-axis aid** — reticle at the principal point, palm-centre marker, and
  the offset shown **in degrees**, green/amber/red. ⭐ Same idea as the `Rsq`/`Lsq`
  readout that made the roll take holdable; the owner had discarded an earlier
  take because holding a geometry by feel is too hard.
* **A frame counts toward a hold only if on-axis** (default 25 px = 2.58°,
  derived not chosen), but ⚠ **every frame is written with an `on_axis` flag** —
  a take that could not hold the axis must be *visible in the data*, not absent.
* Writes the **corpus JSONL schema unchanged**, so every `analysis/` harness reads
  it, plus `declared_deg`, `position_index`, `palm_offset_px`, `alpha_deg`.

⚠ **Six takes: 3 yaw + 3 pitch.** Three of each is what makes the transfer test
possible at all — build the table on one, apply to another.

### ⭐⭐⭐ AND THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THEM IS NOT THE OPERATOR — a distance-free proof

⛔ The obvious objection is caveat zero: *the hand drifted, so of course the depth
readings climb*. **That objection is closed by construction, not by argument.**

Under a pure change of distance every projected span scales by the same `k`, so
`max4` and `abs/0°` **both equal `k` exactly**. Their RATIO therefore cancels the
arm's motion identically — whatever survives in it cannot be the operator.

**Worst `A/M` over the six takes: 1.142, 1.175, 1.206, 1.243, 1.321, 1.335.**
So up to **34%** of divergence remains after the drift has cancelled out. ⭐ And in
the same direction on every take: the absolute estimator reads too far, the
relative one does not.

> ⛔⛔ **RETRACTED THE SAME DAY — the RATIO is not distance-free, it is
> distance-SQUARED.** `max4` = k but `abs/0°` = 1/k, so the ratio goes as 1/k² and
> a pure 10% retreat fabricates 1.23 on its own. The statistic that cancels is the
> **PRODUCT**, and the corrected worst values are **1.077 … 1.209**. See the
> verification section below.

⚠ The two differ only in **which span they select** — `abs` maximises
`px/NOMINAL_SPAN_M`, `max4` maximises `px/px_at_0°`. So the nominal anthropometry
is picking the wrong span as the palm turns, while a per-user baseline picks the
right one. **That is a fixable defect, not a sensor limit.**

so the anchor must return unless the hand moved; it returns, therefore **the hand
moved by at most ~7%, ≈3.5 cm at 0.5 m**, across a whole seven-pose sweep.

> ⛔⛔ **RETRACTED THE SAME DAY.** The premise is false: at the 180° hold three of
> the four spans come back **8–30% SHORT** and `max4` reports the only one that
> does not. "It returned" therefore means "one span returned", which a ~12% retreat
> produces just as readily. **These takes cannot bound the drift.** See below.

⚠ **This narrows — it does not overturn — the retraction above.** That retraction
was right that *geometry allows* a monotone climb when the hand moves. The
measurement now says the movement was **too small to be most of it**: the drift is
bounded at ~7% while the absolute estimator climbed up to **26.6%**. ⭐ The takes
are better than the operator feared.

### ⛔ §4.1 — MAGNITUDE DOES NOT SEPARATE YAW FROM PITCH. THE SIGN DOES.

Cross-talk of the 0°→90° excursion, |weaker axis| / |stronger axis|:
`Rwl` **0.77** · `Rxy` **0.90** · `Rdiag` **0.96** · `Rbow` **0.92** · `Rarea` 0.36.

⭐ **That null was predicted before the run and is geometry, not a defect**: under
orthographic projection a rigid planar palm scales by `cos(yaw)` horizontally and
`cos(pitch)` vertically, so its shape yields exactly ONE scale-free number and
`Rwl` carries `cos(yaw)/cos(pitch)` — pooled by construction.

⭐⭐ **But the SIGN splits them 3/3 with no overlap**: yaw drives `Rwl` **down**
(−0.87, −0.69, −0.77 in log space), pitch drives it **up** (+0.73, +1.01, +1.18),
monotonically to 90° in all six takes. And the signal is large — `Rwl` travels
0.77 → 0.32 under yaw and 0.71 → 2.33 under pitch.
⛔ **It is a discriminator only while the rotation is about ONE axis at a time.**
A mixed pose puts `cos(yaw)/cos(pitch)` back into one equation with two unknowns.

⛔ **The two cues nominated to break that degeneracy DID NOT.** `Rdiag`
(perspective) and `Rbow` (the 10.6 mm out-of-plane bow) both come back
**sign-inconsistent** across takes — `Rdiag` spikes to 3.740 at one hold and drops
below 1 on another; `Rbow` flips sign somewhere in 90–120° in every take. So
neither delivers the clean second observable, and **§4's table must be 2-D**, which
§4.1 named in advance as a real answer rather than a failure.

⭐ **A side finding worth keeping**: `Rbow`'s flip is consistent in one respect —
it runs ≈−0.11 at 0° and ≈+0.07 at 180° in **all six** takes. That is a
**palm-vs-back** signature, not an axis one, and unlike `signed_palm_area` it is
built from a different triangle. Possible `T1`/`DR-2` input; not pursued here.

### ⛔ 1. THE "DISTANCE-FREE" STATISTIC WAS BACKWARDS — it AMPLIFIED drift

The section above argued that `max4` and `abs/0°` both equal `k` under a pure
change of distance, so their **RATIO** cancels the arm. ⛔ **They do not.** Under a
retreat by factor `k` every span scales by `k`, so

```
max4 = k          abs/0° = 1/k          RATIO = 1/k²        PRODUCT = 1
```

They move in **opposite** directions. The ratio does not cancel drift, it
**squares** it: a pure 10% retreat with no rotation at all fabricates a "1.23"
there — which is most of what was reported as 1.14–1.34.

⭐ **The quantity that cancels is the PRODUCT**, `sel = max4 × (abs/0°)`. It is
1.000 exactly under any pure distance change, and `>= 1` always (the max of a
product is at most the product of the maxes). Verified against a synthetic
pure-distance case before being trusted.

**Corrected values — worst `sel` per take: 1.077, 1.134, 1.141, 1.141, 1.199,
1.209.** So **up to +20.9%**, not 34%. ⚠ And because `sel >= 1` by construction,
its *direction* carries no information — the earlier "and in the same direction on
every take" was structural, not evidence.

⛔ **The second witness under-reports pitch too** — it reads 15° and 29° for a
declared 30° and 60°. Two methods that fail for *different* reasons (Horn through
`z`, the ratio through foreshortening) agree with each other more than either
agrees with the declaration. ⚠ **That makes the DECLARATION the outlier**, not
Horn — and the owner had already warned: *"the angles are approximative."*

### ⛔ AND THE THIRD WITNESS IS BLIND EXACTLY WHERE IT IS NEEDED

The ratio-derived truth has no sensitivity at small pitch: measured
`dR/dθ ≈ 0.001 per degree` in the 0–30° band, so 0.02 of ratio noise is **20–57°**
of angle. It cannot referee that band.

**Consistency of the ratio-derived angle as a fraction of the declared one:**

| axis | declared | dA | dB | dC | spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pitch | 30° | **0.00** | 0.50 | **0.04** | **0.50** |
| pitch | 60° | 0.38 | 0.48 | 0.61 | 0.22 |
| pitch | 90° | 0.69 | 0.76 | 0.81 | 0.12 |

| yaw | 90° | 0.74 | 0.70 | 0.72 | **0.04** |

⛔ At the 30° holds the spread is 0.50–0.69 on BOTH axes — the takes do not
reproduce themselves there. Only the 90° holds are consistent (yaw 0.04).

### ⛔⛔ WHICH ALSO UNDERMINES §4.3's PITCH "+41.4° RECOVERED"

The table is **built** from declared angles on one take and **scored** against
declared angles on another. Where no independent witness can check the declaration,
"recovery" only demonstrates that the table reproduces the declaration — it says
nothing about accuracy. ⭐ If the operator's angle error is reproducible, a table
learns the habit and scores beautifully while correcting nothing.

⚠ So §4.3's pitch verdict is **withdrawn**, not downgraded. The yaw verdict (dead,
mean +2.4°) stands: yaw's declarations are self-consistent at 60–90°, which is the
band that matters, and the failure there is not a ground-truth problem.

### ⭐⭐ WHAT SURVIVES, AND IT IS THE USEFUL PART

1. **Slant/tilt stands**, because it never depended on the declared angle being
   right — `σ₂/σ₁` and the compression direction are measurements of SHAPE. The
   monotonicity on both axes and the orthogonal tilt directions are unaffected by
   anything above.
2. **Yaw saturates**, confirmed twice over: at a declared 90° the ratio recovers
   only 0.70–0.74, consistently across all three depths (spread 0.04). That is the
   hand's thickness, and it is now the best-measured fact in this row.
3. ⛔ **THE TAKES CANNOT GROUND-TRUTH THE 30–60° BAND.** All three witnesses fail
   there: Horn through `z`, the ratio through conditioning, the declaration through
   repeatability.

### ⭐ WHAT A TAKE THAT COULD ANSWER IT LOOKS LIKE

The ground truth must be **measurable, not declared** — the standard `t5j` already
sets. It grounds ROLL by the in-image knuckle-row angle, which needs no depth and
no declaration, and that is why the roll numbers have never been in dispute.

For pitch that means either a **physical jig** fixing the hand at known angles, or
a marker whose in-image geometry encodes the angle directly. ⛔ Until one exists,
no pitch claim from these takes — mine included — should be built on.

## Appended 2026-08-27 — ⭐⭐ REGRESSION FROM THE DATA. THE LANDMARK SET MATTERS MORE THAN THE MODEL.

**Owner:** *"start from regression from the data directly, you could avoid this
trap"* … *"finger + trim could help"* … *"make sure the regressions are bijective."*
All three were right, and the first two are now measured.

`analysis/t6_regression_fit.py`.

### ⭐⭐ THE FINDING: FINGERS AND TRIMMING HALVE THE INCONSISTENCY

Cross-take spread of `σ₂/σ₁` at matched declared angles — lower means the feature
means the same thing at every distance, which is what a transferable model needs:

| landmark set | trim | spread |
|---|---|---|
| **palm5** (0,5,9,13,17) | — | **0.162** ← what every earlier attempt used |
| palm+pip | — | 0.108 |
| palm+tips | 25% | 0.071 |
| **no-thumb** | **25%** | **0.067** ← 2.4× better |

⭐ The fingers are extra near-rigid points that condition the fit; the **thumb
hurts** (it moves independently); trimming drops whatever articulated anyway.
⛔ **Every previous attempt in this row — mine included — used the five palm points
and nothing else.** The feature was 2.4× noisier than it needed to be, and no
choice of model could have recovered that.

### ⭐ AND THE FOLD IS ASYMMETRIC — TWO BRANCHES, NOT ONE UNFOLDED CURVE

Front 60° reads `σ = 0.513`; back 60° reads `0.633`. The hand is measurably **not
front-back symmetric**, so unfolding 0–180 onto 0–90 destroys real information. The
palm/back sign selects the branch — which is also what makes the map single-valued.

⭐ **Bijectivity, by construction rather than by assumption:**

    σ₂/σ₁        -> |angle from square|, monotone fit PER BRANCH
    palm/back    -> which branch            (signed_palm_area, depth-free)
    tilt         -> which axis              (the SVD's minor axis)

### ⛔⛔ AND A RESULT I NEARLY REPORTED AS A WIN

Leave-one-DEPTH-out scored **1.2° against Horn's 10.2°**. It is meaningless: the
grid is 30° and the held-out angle is present at the *other* depths, so a monotone
fit **BINS** to it instead of interpolating. ⭐ Only holding out the ANGLE — unseen
at every depth — asks the model to interpolate.

**Leave-one-angle-out, two branches, one scoring rule (`|estimate − declared|`):**

| held out | YAW model | YAW Horn | PITCH model | PITCH Horn |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30° | **4.1°** | 12.3° | **15.5°** | 21.4° |
| 60° | **1.7°** | 17.1° | **8.6°** | 44.7° |
| 120° | 30.0° | **10.8°** | 30.0° | **23.5°** |
| 150° | 13.4° | **5.9°** | **30.0°** | 33.1° |
| mean | 12.3° | **11.5°** | **21.0°** | 30.7° |

⭐⭐ **READ IT BY HALF, NOT BY THE MEAN. The model wins the PALM-FACING half on both
axes and loses the BACK half on both.** At a declared 60° of yaw it predicts within
**1.7°** where Horn is out by **17.1°** — and 60–90° palm-facing is exactly where
the show-stopper lives.

⚠ Two deductions were offered for the losing half: fewer training angles, and `T1`
(back-of-hand landmark quality). ⛔ **BOTH WERE WRONG — see the correction below.**

⛔ **The labels remain the weak link.** The declared angles were measured
irreproducible at the 30° holds (spread 0.50–0.69), so a small error means the
model reproduces the OPERATOR. ⭐ It is a **comparative** result — same labels, same
scoring, both estimators — which is what makes it usable despite that.

### ⛔⛔ CORRECTION, SAME DAY — THE BACK HALF LOST BECAUSE OF MY DATA PARTITION

**Owner:** *"Analyze why it looses on the back half. There is no reason it should."*
⭐ Correct, and the reason was mine, not the hand's.

Splitting the branches **exclusively** at 90° put that hold in the FRONT branch and
left the back with only `{120, 150, 180}`. Hold out 120 and the model trains on
`{150, 180}` — whose σ span does **not** contain 120's σ:

| take | train σ span (150,180) | 120 needs | |
|---|---|---|---|
| dA | 0.847 – 0.932 | **0.601** | OUTSIDE |
| dB | 0.835 – 0.897 | **0.618** | OUTSIDE |
| dC | 0.741 – 0.914 | **0.633** | OUTSIDE |

⛔ So the fit had to **EXTRAPOLATE**, and it clamps by design — scoring exactly
`30.0°` every time. ⚠ **It had nothing to do with `T1` or back-of-hand landmark
quality.** I asserted that from plausibility and it was wrong; the partition was
the cause.

✅ **THE MEND: the 90° samples belong to BOTH branches.** That turns the same
prediction from an extrapolation into an interpolation. C0 holds exactly — both
curves pass through the shared knot — and the derivative matches in the only sense
the data supports, since σ has its **minimum** at 90° and both branches approach it
flattening out.

| held out | yaw before | **yaw after** | pitch before | **pitch after** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30° | 4.1° | **4.1°** | 15.5° | **15.5°** |
| 60° | 1.7° | **1.7°** | 8.6° | **8.6°** |
| 120° | 30.0° | **15.5°** | 30.0° | **16.3°** |
| 150° | 13.4° | **13.4°** | 30.0° | **30.0°** |
| **mean** | 12.3° | **8.7°** | 21.0° | **17.6°** |
| Horn | 11.5° | 11.5° | 30.7° | 30.7° |

⭐⭐ **The model now beats Horn on BOTH axes** — 8.7° vs 11.5° and 17.6° vs 30.7° —
and the front half is untouched.

⚠ **A stiffer C1 form was tried and rejected by measurement.** Fitting
`σ = v + a·u² + b·u³` per branch (`u = θ − 90`) gives exact C1 with zero slope at
90 by construction. It fixed the extrapolation too, but **cost the front half
badly** — yaw 30° went 4.1 → 11.2 and yaw 60° went 1.7 → 6.4 — because two
parameters per branch cannot follow the real curve. ⭐ **Flexibility was doing more
work than smoothness**, so the monotone piecewise fit stays and the mend is the
shared knot rather than a shared polynomial.

⭐ The C1 property still matters for a different reason worth recording: the
palm/back sign that PICKS the branch is least reliable exactly at edge-on, so a
discontinuity there would turn sign flicker into an angle jump. A shared knot means
both branches agree where the decision is worst.

### ⛔⛔ AND THE FINGER GAIN DOES NOT SURVIVE A GRIPPING HAND — the owner asked the right question

**Owner:** *"What happens if the fingers are not exactly extended as in the 6 takes
but are slightly or completely bent?"*

⛔ **The six ratio takes hold an OPEN hand. The game holds a GRIPPING hand.** The
regression is fitted on a pose the game never uses, and the finger features are
exactly what breaks.

Measured: frame-to-frame movement of `σ₂/σ₁` on frames where the PALM barely
rotated (< 1°), so anything left is articulation or noise. **p95 |Δσ| per frame:**

| takes | no-thumb + fingers | palm5 only |
|---|---|---|
| OPEN hand (ratio-calib) | 0.023 – 0.035 | 0.020 – 0.024 |
| **GRIPPING (f1_rig, 15 takes)** | **0.013 – 0.458** | 0.004 – 0.070 |

⭐ **Scale: 30° of real yaw moves σ by ~0.20, i.e. ~0.0067 per degree.** So on a
gripping hand the finger-inclusive feature injects a typical **~6° of spurious
angle per frame**, and up to **68°** on the worst take — while the palm-only
feature stays an order of magnitude tighter.

⛔ **So the 2.4× improvement from including the fingers is real FOR THE TAKES and
does not transfer to the pose the game runs in.** On an open hand the two feature
sets are comparable; under grip the fingers are a liability.

⚠ Note the palm-only feature also degrades under grip (0.024 → up to 0.070),
because gripping changes the palm's own shape a little and the canonical came from
a different pose.

### ⭐ WHAT THAT MAKES THE REAL NEXT STEP

⛔ **Not a build.** The regression cannot enter the pipeline as it stands: it is
fitted on an open hand and would be applied to a closed one. Three ways out, in
increasing cost:

1. **Re-fit on the grip pose.** Needs declared angles WHILE GRIPPING — a take the
   corpus does not contain. ⚠ And the owner has said no more takes for now.
2. **Palm-only features, accepting the noise.** Costs the 2.4×, and the palm-only
   feature is still 3× noisier under grip than on the open takes.
3. **A pose-invariant feature** — one whose value does not move when the fingers
   articulate. That is what the trim was trying to buy and only partly does.

⭐ The honest position: **the orientation result is established for an open hand
and unestablished for a gripping one**, and nothing should be built into the
pipeline until that gap is closed.

### ⭐⭐ THE OWNER'S ARCHITECTURE — FREEZE A MATRIX AT GRAB — IS VALIDATED

**Owner, 2026-08-27:** *"the open hand takes provide the regression fits … On top
of that, multiply by a matrix which is frozen at the moment of grab which captures
the fingers state at the grab. During the later movement, the fingertips movements
will input cube's movement on top of this."*

⭐ It is the pattern this pipeline already uses three times — `DepthRatioTracker`,
`Horn(mode='ref')` and `tip_trim` each freeze a per-grab baseline so an unknown
cancels. This extends it to the POSE.

⭐⭐ **AND THERE IS A REASON IT WORKS THAT WAS NOT OBVIOUS: the cube's rotation is
already grab-relative** (`delta = q_now · conj(q_grab)`), so an ABSOLUTE error at
the grab instant CANCELS. Only relative accuracy matters, which is exactly what a
grab-frozen canonical delivers.

⚠ **The composition is multiplicative, not additive** — and getting this wrong is
what made the first test fail. Compression measured against a canonical sitting at
angle `a₀` is `cos(b)/cos(a₀)`, not `cos(b−a₀)`:

    sigma_abs(b) = sigma_rel(b | a0)  x  sigma_abs(a0)

so: measure `σ_rel` against the frozen canonical, multiply by the grab-time `σ`,
invert through the fitted curve to get the ABSOLUTE angle, subtract `a₀`.
⛔ Fed straight into the same curve without composing, the scheme scored 20.3° on
yaw (worse than Horn's 11.7°). Composed correctly:

| canonical frozen at | yaw MODEL | yaw HORN | pitch MODEL | pitch HORN |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the square pose | 8.7° | 11.5° | 17.6° | 30.7° |
| **an arbitrary pose (30°/60°)** | **10.6°** | 11.7° | **17.4°** | 25.9° |

⭐ Freezing at an arbitrary pose costs ~2° on yaw and nothing on pitch, and still
beats Horn on both axes. **The mechanism is sound.**

### ⭐ WHICH SETTLES THE FEATURE QUESTION TOO

The grip-pose measurement said the finger-inclusive feature jitters 0.013–0.458 per
frame under grip against palm5's 0.004–0.070. ⭐ Combined with the result above,
the architecture that follows is:

1. **At grab** — estimate the absolute `σ₀` against the OPEN-hand canonical. The
   fingers are curled and this is wrong by some amount; ⭐ it does not matter,
   because the error cancels in the grab-relative delta.
2. **Freeze** the grab-time hand as the working canonical (the owner's matrix).
3. **Per frame** — `σ_rel` on the **PALM landmarks only**, compose, invert,
   subtract. Palm-only because under grip it is an order of magnitude steadier.
4. **The fingers keep their own channel** — the grip point, and the owner's *"on
   top of this"* — rather than contaminating the orientation feature.

⚠ **One honest tension**: the multiplicative composition assumes the orthographic
`cos` model, which is the very assumption the regression exists to avoid. It is
re-entering through the composition step. ⭐ The fully empirical alternative is a
2-D fit `(σ_rel, σ₀) → b` learned from the takes directly — enough hold PAIRS exist
to fit it — and that is the version to build if the composition proves brittle.

⛔ **Still untested**: freezing at an actual GRIPPING pose. The takes are open-hand
only, so the composition is validated and the grip-pose canonical is not.

### ✅ THE COMPOSITION IS NOW FITTED, NOT ASSUMED — and it beats Horn on both axes

`analysis/t6_composition_fit.py`. The multiplicative step was the last place the
orthographic `cos` model was smuggled back in. It is now generalised to

    u = sigma_rel^alpha * sigma_0^beta        angle = f(u), f monotone

with `alpha`, `beta` and `f` all fitted. ⭐ `alpha = beta = 1` IS the multiplicative
model, so it is inside the search space and the data was free to keep it.

**It did not.** Fitting the exponents beats the fixed product in every case:

| feature | axis | fitted | alpha=beta=1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| palm5 | yaw | **21.9°** | 23.6° |
| palm5 | pitch | **19.0°** | 25.4° |
| no-thumb | yaw | **19.3°** | 25.0° |
| no-thumb | pitch | **20.8°** | 25.8° |

⭐ So the orthographic product WAS costing accuracy, and the fitted exponents are
the empirical correction. ⚠ Note the palm-only and finger feature sets are now
roughly a wash (21.9 vs 19.3) — far less separation than the 2.4× on the raw
feature spread — so the palm-only choice costs little and keeps the grip-pose
stability.

### ⭐⭐ AND THE REMAINING ERROR IS ALL IN A REGIME THE GAME NEVER ENTERS

| | all front pairs | grab NOT edge-on (σ₀ > 0.35) | Horn |
|---|---|---|---|
| yaw | 21.9° | **9.2°** | 13.2° |
| pitch | 19.0° | **9.8°** | 24.3° |

⛔ Freezing the canonical near **edge-on** is the degenerate case: σ₀ ≈ 0.04–0.29
there, so `σ_rel × σ₀` is tiny and the inversion is ill-conditioned. Exactly the
degeneracy the strategy draft predicted.

⭐ **And it is not special pleading: the pipeline already refuses to snap when
edge-on** (`palm_depth.SNAP_REQUIRES_VALID_DEPTH` and `DR-2`'s band). A grab frozen
there does not occur in the game, so the excluded pairs are outside the operating
regime — and the guard that excludes them is already shipped.

⭐ **Net: ~9–10° on BOTH axes against Horn's 13.2° and 24.3°**, with the
composition fitted rather than assumed, palm-only features, and bijectivity by
construction.

⚠ Still on declared labels. The independent-truth check (`t5j`/`t5h`, whose ground
truth owes nothing to a declaration) is the next step and has not been run.
